# ⚡ 48-Hour Field Mastery

> An MIT grad student's method: use LLMs + structured questioning to go from zero to conversational mastery in a new field within 48 hours. The tool doesn't matter — the questions do.

## Background

Most students spend an entire semester just figuring out what a field is really about. This method compresses that into 48 hours through three rounds of structured prompting.

---

## Method: Three Rounds of Questioning

### Preparation

Don't just upload one textbook. Gather as much material as possible for context:

- Multiple core textbooks
- Key academic papers
- Course lecture notes / slides

Upload everything into a long-context LLM (NotebookLM, Claude, etc.).

---

### Round 1: Extract Mental Models (~20 min)

**Prompt:**

```
What are the 5 core mental models that all experts in this field share?

Not "summarize the material." Not "explain this concept."
I want the mental models — the things professors take years to internalize.
```

**Purpose:** You're not asking for a list of topics. You're asking for the underlying frameworks experts use to think about problems.

---

### Round 2: Map the Controversies (~20 min)

**Prompt:**

```
Now tell me: on which 3 key points do experts in this field fundamentally disagree, and what are the strongest arguments on each side?
```

**Purpose:** In 20 minutes you get a complete knowledge map of the field — the controversies, the consensus, and the open questions, all at a glance.

---

### Round 3: Deep Understanding Test (~6 hours)

**Prompt:**

```
Generate 10 questions that can distinguish whether someone truly understands the material deeply versus just memorized it.
```

Then work through these questions one by one, referencing all your materials.

**For every wrong answer, follow up with:**

```
Explain where I went wrong and what I missed.
```

---

### Result

After 48 hours, you can hold a fluent conversation with your advisor without getting stumped.

## Core Insight

> Most people use LLMs as a fancy highlighter.
> Top students treat them as a private tutor who has read every paper in the field.
>
> **The gap between a semester and 48 hours isn't about how much you study — it's about whether you ask the right questions.**
